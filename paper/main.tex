\abstract{
Indoor localization commonly combines WiFi received signal strength indicator (RSSI) fingerprints with inertial measurements. However, these sensor streams are sampled asynchronously at different rates; therefore, observations rarely align in time and may be missing or stale. Most existing methods resample the data onto a shared clock and process each modality using a separate branch. We instead present a continuous-time transformer that represents observations as an unordered set of timestamped tokens. Each token encodes its measurement, modality, and elapsed time since acquisition. A single attention block jointly fuses information across modalities and time, avoiding both resampling and modality-specific branches while remaining invariant to token order. During training, we randomly mask entire modalities and individual observations, enabling the model to operate under missing or outdated inputs. We validate the modality-specific encoders on their respective benchmarks and evaluate the fused model on both simulated and real datasets, including a cross-session dataset collected on unseen days. The proposed approach improves upon an open WiFi fingerprinting baseline under cross-session drift, remains competitive with learned fusion and dead-reckoning baselines, and degrades gracefully as observations become missing or stale. These results suggest that a single continuous-time transformer provides a practical framework for indoor localization from asynchronous sensors.
}

\section{\uppercase{Conclusion}}\label{sec:conclusion}

\noindent We presented in this paper a continuous-time Set-Transformer for asynchronous WiFi-IMU indoor localization. Each observation is represented as a token with a learned encoding of its elapsed time, and a single permutation-invariant self-attention block fuses the resulting set. Modality and instant dropout make robustness to missing and stale observations a property of the training procedure rather than the architecture itself. On standard single-modality benchmarks, the WiFi encoder outperforms an open-source fingerprinting baseline, while the lightweight inertial encoder remains competitive with a significantly larger reference model despite using fewer parameters. The fused model also outperforms an open WiFi reference method under real cross-session drift, the setting most representative of practical deployment, while degrading gracefully in the presence of missing or stale inputs.

The approach has several limitations. The simulated WiFi environment is synthetic, making its sub-meter accuracy optimistic. The inertial encoder still underperforms a significantly larger reference model on cross-subject trajectories, and the cross-session margin is influenced, in part, by the composition of the test split. Future work will focus on integrating additional sensing modalities to improve robustness, as well as incorporating physical constraints into both the fusion mechanism and the training process.
