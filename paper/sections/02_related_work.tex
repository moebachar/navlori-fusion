\section{\uppercase{Related Work and Motivation}}\label{sec:related}

\noindent WiFi received signal strength indicator (RSSI) fingerprints recover absolute
indoor position by matching a live scan to a labeled survey~\cite{bahl2000radar}.
On UJIIndoorLoc~\cite{torressospedra2014ujiindoorloc}, a 1-NN reference method
reports a 7.9~m error. Such fingerprinting stays single-modality, encodes neither
elapsed time nor inertial motion, and drifts as environment and hardware change,
which motivates pairing WiFi with a dense motion modality.

Learned inertial navigation provides dense relative estimates.
RoNIN~\cite{yan2019ronin} is the standard benchmark, and attention entered the
field through iMoT~\cite{nguyen2025imot}, which uses accelerometer and gyroscope
channels. Yet the sampling rate is fixed by the window length or token dimension,
and inertial tracking drifts due to the absence of absolute reference; combining WiFi's
absolute reference with inertial motion is the remedy.

The fusion of these two heterogeneous streams is well studied, but mostly through
a separate branch per modality, merged by a classical filter or late
concatenation. WIO-EKF, evaluated across a ten-day session
gap~\cite{zhou2024wioekf}, keeps each sensor in its own EKF branch. Deep variants
concatenate recurrent streams or substitute a null vector when WiFi is
absent~\cite{wei2021sensorfusion}, and where attention appears it sits inside a
branch rather than as the fusion itself. Two limits recur: streams are resampled
onto a common grid before fusion, and robustness to a missing sensor is hand-set
or filter-based rather than learned. Evaluation also tends to use random or
cross-user splits, leaving generalization across sessions lightly tested.

The ingredients for a different representation already exist, scattered across
domains. The Transformer's positional encoding assumes equidistant
positions~\cite{vaswani2017transformer}, which breaks for multi-rate sensors,
whereas set models are permutation-invariant over unordered
inputs~\cite{lee2019settransformer}. Dropping an entire modality at training time,
the precedent for robustness to an absent channel, originates with
ModDrop~\cite{neverova2014moddrop}. The learned embedding of real-valued time
builds on Time2Vec and mTAN~\cite{kazemi2019time2vec,shukla2021mtan}. SeFT and
STraTS realize a single continuous-time set transformer without an ODE solver, but
for clinical data~\cite{horn2020seft,tipirneni2022strats}. Two differences set the
proposed method apart from STraTS, beyond the domain shift. It adds an encoder per
modality that maps heterogeneous WiFi RSSI scans and inertial windows into the
shared token, where STraTS assumes scalar clinical observations. It also trains
with modality and instant dropout for robustness across sessions, which STraTS
does not address. The closest localisation
assemblies remain partial: AFT-VO fuses asynchronous sources in one transformer
but is camera-only and bins time~\cite{kaygusuz2022aftvo}, while ContiFormer puts
an ODE solver inside attention at high cost~\cite{chen2023contiformer}.

Three properties separate these assemblies, and each holds only a subset.
P1 is continuous-time tokens that avoid resampling and an ODE solver; P2 is one
permutation-invariant set transformer doing cross-modal and cross-time fusion in
a single self-attention block; P3 is learned robustness to missing or stale
sensors, validated across sessions. Table~\ref{tab:properties} scores the methods
discussed above against the three.

The proposed method completes the combination for asynchronous WiFi--IMU
localization. Each observation carries a learned sinusoidal encoding of its
real-valued elapsed $\Delta t$, a lightweight alternative to resampling and
ODE-in-attention. One set transformer performs cross-modal and cross-time fusion
in a single self-attention block. Modality and instant dropout extend the earlier
whole-modality scheme to the (modality, time) token set, giving graceful
degradation under missing and stale sensors that evaluation across sessions
confirms.
