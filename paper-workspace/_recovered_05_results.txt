3	\subsection{Per-Leg Encoder Validation}
4	
5	\noindent
6	Before the two streams are fused, each encoder is measured against its field's
7	standard benchmark, so any later fusion result rests on encoders already
8	tested in isolation. Table~\ref{tab:perleg} collects both comparisons.
9	
10	On UJIIndoorLoc, WiFi-Net reaches an MAE of $8.69$~m on the validation scans,
11	against $15.17$~m for wlan\_localization, a reduction of $42.7\%$.
12	The benchmark fixes $520$ access points and carries no trajectory structure,
13	so the comparison isolates the fingerprint-to-position mapping with no
14	temporal context to lean on. Under that constraint WiFi-Net more than halves
15	the reference error, establishing the WiFi leg as the stronger absolute-position
16	estimator on this benchmark.
17	
18	The inertial leg is the harder case. On RoNIN canonical, IMUCNN attains a raw
19	ATE of $9.72$~m on the $32$ held-out sequences, against $5.14$~m for ResNet1D,
20	so the lighter encoder trails the reference by $89.2\%$.
21	The two encoders are not the same size: IMUCNN carries about $0.05$~M
22	parameters against ResNet1D's $4.6$~M, a factor of roughly $95$. The gap is an
23	in-domain-competitive encoder paying a cross-subject generalization cost at a
24	far smaller parameter budget. Figure~\ref{fig:ronin_traj} shows the qualitative
25	consequence on one held-out sequence: IMUCNN tracks the route but drifts more
26	than ResNet1D over the leg.
27	
28	The aggregate gap, however, hides a per-sequence picture that is less
29	one-sided. Figure~\ref{fig:ronin_perseq} ranks IMUCNN's five best held-out
30	sequences by raw ATE against the ResNet1D mean of $5.14$~m: four of them fall
31	below that mean, with only the fifth just above it. The lighter encoder is
32	therefore competitive sequence by sequence, and the $89.2\%$ aggregate gap is
33	driven by the harder remainder rather than a uniform deficit across the split.

62	\subsection{End-to-End Fusion}
63	
64	\noindent
65	With both encoders validated, the set-transformer fuses them into a single
66	position estimate, evaluated first in the controlled simulation, then on the
67	cross-session real-world collection that carries the headline claim.
68	Table~\ref{tab:fusion} collects the fusion results against the three named
69	baselines.
70	
71	In the Webots simulation, the two-modality model at $K=4$ reaches an MAE of
72	$0.441$~m on test, below the half-metre target. No public two-modality method
73	targets this simulation, so the number stands as a controlled-lab sanity check:
74	fusion recovers sub-metre position once the WiFi field is clean, the condition
75	the simulation is built to provide. Figure~\ref{fig:webots_scatter} shows the
76	predicted positions against ground truth on the test paths.
77	
78	MSILN site1/B1 removes that condition. On the held-out test session the
79	set-transformer reaches $10.90$~m MAE, against $28.31$~m for
80	wlan\_localization, $12.49$~m for PDR-from-start and $52.69$~m for IMUWiFine.
81	It wins against all three: $-61.5\%$ over wlan\_localization, $-12.7\%$ over
82	PDR-from-start and $-79.3\%$ over IMUWiFine.
83	On the validation session the margin is narrow: $16.67$~m for the
84	set-transformer against $16.88$~m for PDR-from-start, with wlan\_localization
85	at $21.26$~m and IMUWiFine at $65.15$~m.
86	The decisive separation appears on the test session, recorded on days the model
87	never observed: where wlan\_localization nearly doubles its error from
88	validation to test, the fusion error falls, because fusing the dense inertial
89	stream against the sparse WiFi anchor holds position across the session
90	boundary. IMUWiFine, a learned WiFi--IMU fusion in its own right, fails to
91	generalize cross-session here, staying above $52$~m on both splits---an
92	informative negative result for learned fusion under fingerprint drift.
93	The training curves in Figure~\ref{fig:msiln_curves} make this concrete: the
94	proposed model drives its validation MAE down to roughly $16$~m, while the
95	IMUWiFine LSTM plateaus near $65$~m and never fits the cross-session mapping.
96	Figure~\ref{fig:msiln_cdf} reports the full per-sample error distribution on
97	the test session, and Figure~\ref{fig:msiln_perpath} illustrates, on the two
98	paths where the proposed method wins, how its per-path error sits below
99	PDR-from-start; this is a per-path view of those paths, not the full split.

151	\subsection{Generalization to a Further Dataset}
152	
153	\noindent
154	The cross-session result on MSILN exposed the IMUWiFine LSTM as a poor
155	generalizer across sessions, but that collection is not the LSTM's home ground.
156	IMUWiFine floor~4~\cite{nurpeiissov2022imuwifine} is, so it provides the
157	complementary test: a within-distribution held-out-path split on the LSTM's own
158	dataset, where the proposed method has no session-drift advantage to exploit.
159	The proposed method reaches a test MAE of $6.37$~m on this split, against a
160	validation MAE of $2.31$~m.
161	
162	Figure~\ref{fig:iwfine_showcase} shows four paths where the proposed method is best or competitive, under three
163	methods: wlan\_localization, the IMUWiFine LSTM trained on this dataset's own
164	data, and the proposed method. On its home ground the LSTM is strong---the same
165	model that plateaued near $52$--$65$~m on MSILN tracks these paths to roughly
166	one metre---and the proposed method matches it, winning two of the four showcase
167	paths (path~$69$ at $1.06$~m and path~$64$ at $0.84$~m) and trailing narrowly on
168	the other two. Both learned methods beat wlan\_localization by roughly two to
169	three times on every path. The reading is consistent rather than contradictory:
170	the LSTM generalizes within its own distribution but not across sessions,
171	whereas the proposed method is competitive within distribution \emph{and} wins
172	the cross-session MSILN test. This further dataset therefore corroborates the
173	method's generalization claim rather than undermining it.

186	\subsection{Robustness and Ablations}
187	
188	\noindent
189	The remaining experiments run on the Webots simulation, where every signal is
190	controlled and individual sensors can be suppressed or aged on demand.
191	
192	\textbf{Modality dropout.} Suppressing a modality at test time exposes what
193	each stream contributes. With WiFi only the test MAE is $0.561$~m; with IMU
194	only it is $3.491$~m; with both it is $0.441$~m.
195	The WiFi token anchors absolute position, so dropping IMU costs little, while
196	IMU alone drifts without an absolute reference. Fusion degrades gracefully
197	rather than failing when one stream is absent.
198	
199	\textbf{Staleness.} Aging the WiFi token while the IMU stream stays fresh maps
200	the cost of asynchronous, delayed measurements. As the WiFi token age grows
201	from $0$ to $4$ instants, the test MAE rises $0.441 \to 0.587 \to 0.772 \to
202	0.980 \to 3.491$~m.
203	Small lags cost little; the sharp rise comes only once the WiFi anchor is fully
204	stale, turning what would be a cliff into a slope across moderate delays.
205	
206	\textbf{Latency.} On the Quadro P4000 the model runs at $4.77$~ms per sample at
207	batch size $1$ and $0.146$~ms per sample at batch size $32$, fast enough for
208	real-time use.

210	\subsection{Limitations}
211	
212	\noindent
213	The inertial encoder trails the reference on its own benchmark. On RoNIN
214	canonical IMUCNN reports a raw ATE of $9.72$~m against ResNet1D's $5.14$~m
215	($+89.2\%$); removing a rigid-body offset (Umeyama alignment) narrows the gap
216	to $7.62$~m ($+48.2\%$).
217	Even with alignment the gap remains, and it reflects a $95\times$-smaller
218	encoder paying a cross-subject generalization cost; closing it at this
219	parameter budget is open work.
220	
221	The MSILN test split is not uniform. One path contributes $786$ samples,
222	roughly $28\%$ of the test mass, and is WiFi-dense, so its WiFi-favourable
223	composition shapes the aggregate test figure.
224	The cross-session margin reported above holds against all three named
225	baselines, but the dominance of this single path is the reason the test figure
226	should be read alongside the validation figure, not in isolation.
