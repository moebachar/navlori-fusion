# Verified References & SOTA Survey: Deep Inertial Odometry / Pedestrian Dead-Reckoning (2020–2025) for an ICINCO 2026 Continuous-Time Set-Transformer Paper

## TL;DR
- All three core references (RoNIN, IONet, TLIO) are **REAL and verified** against canonical records. The task description contained three minor author-name errors, corrected below: TLIO's third author is **Eddy Ilg** (not "Eric Ilg"); IONet's second author is **Chris Xiaoxuan Lu**; and "Niki Trigoni" is the form of Agathoniki/Niki Trigoni.
- A stronger learned-inertial baseline than RoNIN ResNet1D **should be acknowledged, but with care**: on the RoNIN dataset itself, RoNIN ResNet remains surprisingly competitive (it has the lowest seen-subject ATE in CTIN's own table, and still beats iMoT on T-RTE). The most defensible "newer SOTA" additions are **CTIN (AAAI 2022), iMoT (AAAI 2025), and EqNIO (ICLR 2025)**.
- The widely circulated claim that "CTIN reduces ATE ~46% / RTE ~32% over RoNIN" is **NOT supported** by CTIN's own paper, which reports an average ATE improvement over RoNIN ResNet of **21.78% (seen) / 3.97% (unseen)** — do not repeat the inflated figure.

## Key Findings
- **RoNIN** was published at **ICRA 2020** (DOI 10.1109/ICRA40945.2020.9196860, pp. 3146–3152); **arXiv:1905.12853** is the 2019 preprint. The canonical published author order is Herath, Yan, Furukawa (the arXiv/Semantic Scholar record lists Yan, Herath, Furukawa — a discrepancy worth noting).
- **IONet** is **AAAI 2018**, DOI 10.1609/aaai.v32i1.12102, vol. 32 no. 1, pp. 6468–6476; arXiv:1802.02209.
- **TLIO** is **RA-L 2020**, vol. 5, no. 4, pp. 5653–5660, DOI 10.1109/LRA.2020.3007421; arXiv:2007.01867.
- RoNIN ResNet is a 1-D ResNet-18 + 512-unit FC head with **≈4.63 M parameters** — roughly **90× larger** than the paper's ~0.05 M-param 1-D CNN IMU encoder. The strong efficiency contrast is a genuine selling point.
- True continuous-time / Neural-ODE inertial work is **thin** in the pedestrian-IMU space; CTIN's "Contextual Transformer" naming is NOT a continuous-time model, so the paper's Δt-encoding contribution occupies a relatively open niche.

## Details

### (a) RoNIN — VERIFIED
**(1) Metadata:** Sachini Herath, Hang Yan, Yasutaka Furukawa. "RoNIN: Robust Neural Inertial Navigation in the Wild: Benchmark, Evaluations, & New Methods." In *2020 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA)*, pp. 3146–3152. IEEE, 2020. DOI: 10.1109/ICRA40945.2020.9196860. Preprint: arXiv:1905.12853 (2019). The exact title (with "Benchmark, Evaluations, & New Methods") is confirmed. **Relationship to preprint:** arXiv:1905.12853 is the earlier version of the same work; the ICRA 2020 proceedings paper is the canonical citation. Note the author-order discrepancy: the IEEE published version and project website (ronin.cs.sfu.ca) list **Herath, Yan, Furukawa**, whereas the arXiv/Semantic Scholar metadata lists **Yan, Herath, Furukawa** — cite the published order.
**(2) Summary:** RoNIN establishes a large benchmark — "the largest inertial navigation database consisting of more than 42.7 hours of IMU and ground-truth 3D motion data from 100 human subjects" — and proposes heading-agnostic neural architectures (ResNet, LSTM, TCN) that regress velocity in a stabilized coordinate frame, handling arbitrary phone orientations and placements. The ResNet variant ("RoNIN ResNet") became the field's de-facto baseline.
**(3) Relation/group:** **Inertial/IMU.** RoNIN ResNet is the primary baseline the paper's lightweight IMU encoder should be benchmarked against; our paper differs by being multimodal (asynchronous WiFi + IMU) continuous-time fusion rather than IMU-only velocity regression.

### (b) IONet — VERIFIED
**(1) Metadata:** Changhao Chen, Chris Xiaoxuan Lu, Andrew Markham, Niki (Agathoniki) Trigoni. "IONet: Learning to Cure the Curse of Drift in Inertial Odometry." *Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence* (Thirty-Second AAAI Conference, AAAI-18), vol. 32, no. 1, pp. 6468–6476, 2018. DOI: 10.1609/aaai.v32i1.12102. Preprint: arXiv:1802.02209. Title confirmed exactly. **Author-name notes:** the task's "Xiaoxuan Lu" is more fully **Chris Xiaoxuan Lu**; "Niki Trigoni" and "Agathoniki Trigoni" denote the same person.
**(2) Summary:** IONet reframes inertial tracking as a sequential learning problem, segmenting IMU data into independent windows and using a deep LSTM to regress polar displacement (Δheading, Δlocation) per window, breaking the cycle of continuous error integration. It generalizes to non-periodic motions (shopping trolley, baby stroller) that defeat step-based PDR.
**(3) Relation/group:** **Inertial/IMU** (the foundational deep-learning inertial-odometry paper). Our paper similarly abandons hand-crafted PDR but uses attention/set-based fusion of two asynchronous modalities rather than an LSTM on IMU windows alone.

### (c) TLIO — VERIFIED
**(1) Metadata:** Wenxin Liu, David Caruso, **Eddy Ilg**, Jing Dong, Anastasios I. Mourikis, Kostas Daniilidis, Vijay Kumar, Jakob Engel. "TLIO: Tight Learned Inertial Odometry." *IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L)*, vol. 5, no. 4, pp. 5653–5660, October 2020. DOI: 10.1109/LRA.2020.3007421. Preprint: arXiv:2007.01867. **Correction:** the task lists "Eric Ilg"; the correct author is **Eddy Ilg**. As an RA-L paper it was eligible for presentation at IROS 2020, but the canonical citation is RA-L vol. 5, no. 4.
**(2) Summary:** TLIO pairs a ResNet that regresses 3D displacement and its uncertainty with a stochastic-cloning Extended Kalman Filter, tightly fusing the learned relative-motion measurement to estimate pose, velocity, and IMU biases from IMU-only data (trained on ~60 h of headset pedestrian data).
**(3) Relation/group:** **Inertial/IMU**, with multimodal fusion via filtering. Our paper performs learned multimodal fusion (WiFi + IMU) end-to-end with attention rather than feeding a learned displacement into an EKF.

### (d) State-of-the-Art Survey, 2020–2025

**IDOL (AAAI 2021) — VERIFIED.** Scott Sun, Dennis Melamed, Kris Kitani. "IDOL: Inertial Deep Orientation-Estimation and Localization." *Proc. AAAI Conf. on Artificial Intelligence*, vol. 35, no. 7, pp. 6128–6137, 2021. DOI: 10.1609/aaai.v35i7.16763. arXiv:2102.04024. A two-stage data-driven pipeline (RNN + EKF for orientation, then a position network) on commodity smartphones, treating orientation error as a major source of localization error; ships the 20+ h IDOL dataset. *Group:* inertial/IMU. *Differs:* IMU-only, two-stage, no WiFi.

**RNIN-VIO (ISMAR 2021) — VERIFIED.** Danpeng Chen, Nan Wang, Runsen Xu, Weijian Xie, Hujun Bao, Guofeng Zhang. "RNIN-VIO: Robust Neural Inertial Navigation Aided Visual-Inertial Odometry in Challenging Scenes." *2021 IEEE International Symposium on Mixed and Augmented Reality (ISMAR)*, pp. 275–283, 2021. DOI: 10.1109/ISMAR52148.2021.00044. A tightly-coupled EKF fusing a robust neural inertial network (RNIN; ResNet + LSTM) with visual-inertial odometry; extends TLIO's idea to continuous human motion with a combined displacement/velocity loss. *Group:* inertial/IMU + multimodal fusion. *Differs:* visual (not WiFi) fusion, filter-based.

**CTIN (AAAI 2022) — VERIFIED.** Bingbing Rao, Ehsan Kazemi, Yifan Ding, Devu M. Shila, Frank M. Tucker, Liqiang Wang. "CTIN: Robust Contextual Transformer Network for Inertial Navigation." *Proc. AAAI Conf. on Artificial Intelligence*, vol. 36, no. 5, pp. 5413–5421, 2022. DOI: 10.1609/aaai.v36i5.20479. arXiv:2112.02143. A ResNet-based encoder enhanced with local and global multi-head self-attention, a Transformer decoder fusing spatial and temporal representations, and multi-task uncertainty-weighted learning of velocity/trajectory; evaluated on RIDI, OxIOD, RoNIN, IDOL, and an in-house set. *Group:* attention/transformer + inertial. **Critical nuance:** CTIN states verbatim, "CTIN improves an average ATE on all seen test datasets by 34.74%, **21.78%**, and 37.46% over R-LSTM, R-ResNet, and R-TCN, respectively; the corresponding numbers for all unseen test datasets are 34.73%, **3.97%**, and 29.47%." On the RoNIN dataset itself, CTIN's own table reports that "**R-ResNet reports the smallest ATE of 3.99 for RoNIN-seen**" — i.e., CTIN does *not* clearly beat RoNIN ResNet on RoNIN. *Relation:* closest transformer predecessor; our paper differs via set-based, permutation-invariant, continuous-time asynchronous WiFi+IMU fusion and a far smaller encoder.

**NILoc / Neural Inertial Localization (CVPR 2022) — VERIFIED.** Sachini Herath, David Caruso, Chen Liu, Yufan Chen, Yasutaka Furukawa. "Neural Inertial Localization." *Proc. IEEE/CVF Conf. on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR)*, pp. 6604–6613, 2022. arXiv:2203.15851. Defines the *inertial localization* task (absolute location from inertial history) and solves it with a Transformer + temporal convolutional network + autoregressive decoder mapping a velocity sequence to a location; ships a 53 h benchmark. *Group:* attention/transformer + inertial localization. The most directly relevant Furukawa-group precedent; differs from our paper by being IMU-only (no WiFi) and discrete-time.

**Fusion-DHL (ICRA 2021) — VERIFIED.** Sachini Herath, Saghar Irandoust, Bowen Chen, Yiming Qian, Pyojin Kim, Yasutaka Furukawa. "Fusion-DHL: WiFi, IMU, and Floorplan Fusion for Dense History of Locations in Indoor Environments." *2021 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA)*, pp. 5677–5683, 2021. DOI: 10.1109/ICRA48506.2021.9561115. arXiv:2105.08837. Multimodal fusion of an IMU relative trajectory, sparse WiFi geolocalization constraints, and a floorplan image via a CNN-based refinement (RMSE ~5 m vs ~12 m for prior standard, at ~50 Hz density). *Group:* **multimodal fusion (WiFi + IMU)** — the closest prior art to our paper's exact modality combination. *Differs:* an optimization + CNN pipeline that relies on floorplans, not an end-to-end attention model, and not continuous-time asynchronous fusion. This is the single most important paper to position against on the "WiFi+IMU fusion" axis.

**IMUNet (IEEE TIM 2024) — VERIFIED.** Behnam Zeinali, Hadi Zandizari, Morris J. Chang. "IMUNet: Efficient Regression Architecture for Inertial IMU Navigation and Positioning." *IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement*, vol. 73, art. 2513413 (IEEE Xplore doc. 10480886), 2024. arXiv:2208.00068 (preprint title slightly differs: "...for IMU Navigation and Positioning"). An edge-efficient architecture using depthwise/pointwise 1-D convolutions (MobileResNet-style blocks); achieves accuracy comparable to RoNIN's ResNet-18 at substantially lower parameter and FLOP cost, evaluated on RoNIN, RIDI, OxIOD and a new ARCore dataset. *Group:* inertial/IMU (efficiency-focused). The closest prior work in spirit to our ~0.05 M-param encoder's efficiency story, though it does not fuse WiFi and is not a large accuracy improvement over RoNIN ResNet (its advantage is efficiency, not accuracy).

**NeurIT (preprint, 2024) — VERIFIED as PREPRINT only.** Xinzhe Zheng, Sijie Ji, Yipeng Pan, Kaiwen Zhang, Chenshu Wu. "NeurIT: Pushing the Limit of Neural Inertial Tracking for Indoor Robotic IoT." arXiv:2404.08939 (v1 Apr 2024; v2 Jul 2025). A Time-Frequency Block-recurrent Transformer (TF-BRT) combining RNN + Transformer with body-frame magnetometer differentiation. It reports verbatim that "it significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines by **48.21% on unseen data**," achieving "an average drift rate of 0.62%" and "a mere 1-meter tracking error over a 300-meter distance" — but note the 48.21% gain is measured against the best baseline (UniTS), not RoNIN ResNet, and the paper reports comparatively weaker results specifically on the RoNIN dataset. *Group:* attention/transformer + inertial. **Flag as a non-peer-reviewed preprint.** *Differs:* robotic-IoT focus, magnetometer reliance, no WiFi.

**iMoT (AAAI 2025) — VERIFIED.** Son Minh Nguyen, Linh Duy Tran, Duc Viet Le, Paul J. M. Havinga. "iMoT: Inertial Motion Transformer for Inertial Navigation." *Proc. AAAI Conf. on Artificial Intelligence*, vol. 39, no. 6, pp. 6209–6217, 2025. DOI: 10.1609/aaai.v39i6.32664. arXiv:2412.12190. A Transformer with a Progressive Series Decoupler, Adaptive Positional Encoding (explicitly handling temporal discrepancies between modalities), and a small set of learnable "query motion particles" (a set-of-queries decoder) modeling per-segment motion uncertainty, plus a dynamic scoring mechanism. It has ~14.49 M parameters (~3× RoNIN ResNet's 4.63 M). It reports surpassing "robust methods that account for motion uncertainties, such as CTIN (6.89 m) and TLIO (6.77 m), by 22.93%" in ATE (iMoT achieving 5.31 m on RoNIN-unseen), while conceding that "RoResNet18 demonstrates slightly better T-RTE and comparable D-RTE results relative to our method." *Group:* **attention/transformer/set + inertial** — the closest conceptual neighbor to our work (query-set decoding, cross-modal adaptive positional encoding). *Differs:* our paper adds true continuous-time Δt encoding, asynchronous WiFi+IMU fusion, and a ~290× smaller IMU encoder.

**EqNIO (ICLR 2025) — VERIFIED.** Royina Karegoudra Jayanth, Yinshuang Xu, Ziyun Wang, Evangelos Chatzipantazis, Kostas Daniilidis, Daniel Gehrig. "EqNIO: Subequivariant Neural Inertial Odometry." *The Thirteenth International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR)*, 2025 (OpenReview id C8jXEugWkq). arXiv:2408.06321. A canonicalization scheme enforcing roto-reflection (O(2)/subequivariant) symmetry around gravity that wraps existing backbones (TLIO, RoNIN) and sets a new IMU-only SOTA on the TLIO, Aria, RIDI and OxIOD datasets by improving generalization across IMU mount orientations and motion directions. *Group:* inertial/IMU (equivariant). *Differs:* symmetry-by-design IMU-only odometry, orthogonal to our multimodal continuous-time fusion — best cited as "current IMU-only SOTA," not as a competitor on the multimodal task.

**Supporting datasets & architecture references (VERIFIED):**
- **RIDI** — Hang Yan, Qi Shan, Yasutaka Furukawa. "RIDI: Robust IMU Double Integration." *Computer Vision – ECCV 2018*, LNCS vol. 11217, pp. 621–636 (some indexes list pp. 641–656 in the Springer volume), Springer, Cham. DOI: 10.1007/978-3-030-01261-8_38. arXiv:1712.09004. The first supervised-learning inertial-navigation method and an early benchmark. *Group:* inertial/IMU dataset+method.
- **OxIOD** — Changhao Chen, Peijun Zhao, Chris Xiaoxuan Lu, Wei Wang, Andrew Markham, Niki Trigoni. "OxIOD: The Dataset for Deep Inertial Odometry." arXiv:1809.07491 (2018). A widely used inertial-odometry benchmark (158 sequences, ~42.5 km, ~14.7 h). **Remains a preprint (CoRR)**; the closely related peer-reviewed journal paper is "Deep Learning based Pedestrian Inertial Navigation: Methods, Dataset and On-Device Inference" (arXiv:2001.04061), which introduces the L-IONet model. *Group:* inertial/IMU dataset.
- **Set Transformer** — Juho Lee, Yoonho Lee, Jungtaek Kim, Adam R. Kosiorek, Seungjin Choi, Yee Whye Teh. "Set Transformer: A Framework for Attention-based Permutation-Invariant Neural Networks." *Proc. 36th International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML)*, PMLR vol. 97, pp. 3744–3753, 2019. arXiv:1810.00825. The foundational permutation-invariant attention module (with inducing-point attention reducing self-attention to linear cost) that underpins a set-transformer fusion design. *Group:* attention/transformer/set.

## Recommendations
**Stage 1 — Baselines (do this regardless).** Keep **RoNIN ResNet1D as a primary baseline** — it is the field standard and, on the RoNIN dataset specifically, still highly competitive (lowest seen-subject ATE in CTIN's table; best T-RTE in iMoT's table). Do not overstate its weakness. Report your ~0.05 M vs RoNIN's ~4.63 M parameter contrast prominently as an efficiency result.

**Stage 2 — Add a stronger transformer baseline.** Include **CTIN (AAAI 2022)** and, if at all feasible, **iMoT (AAAI 2025)**. iMoT is the strongest recent peer-reviewed transformer and shares your query-set decoding idea, so it is the most important method to position against; failing to cite it is the most likely reviewer objection.

**Stage 3 — Frame novelty correctly.** Cite **EqNIO (ICLR 2025)** as the current IMU-only SOTA (via equivariance) and frame your contribution as *orthogonal*: multimodal (async WiFi+IMU) + continuous-time, not a competitor on IMU-only odometry. Cite **Fusion-DHL** as the closest WiFi+IMU prior art and **NILoc** as the closest transformer-based inertial-localization precedent, differentiating on (i) end-to-end attention vs. optimization+CNN, and (ii) continuous-time Δt encoding vs. discrete-time.

**Stage 4 — Continuous-time positioning.** Explicitly distinguish your Δt-encoding from CTIN's "Contextual Transformer" (which is **not** a continuous-time model). The relative scarcity of true continuous-time/Neural-ODE pedestrian-inertial baselines is a genuine gap that *strengthens* your novelty claim — state it.

**Stage 5 — Hygiene.** Correct author names (**Eddy Ilg**, **Chris Xiaoxuan Lu**) and **avoid the unverified "46%/32%" CTIN-vs-RoNIN figure**; use the paper's actual 21.78% (seen) / 3.97% (unseen) numbers if you cite CTIN's gains at all.

**Threshold that changes the plan:** if your method underperforms RoNIN ResNet on the RoNIN test set ATE/RTE, you must report it transparently and pivot the headline claim to the multimodal-accuracy and parameter-efficiency advantages rather than raw IMU-only accuracy.

## Caveats
- **Cross-paper ATE/RTE numbers are not directly comparable.** RoNIN's full dataset is ~50% proprietary, and methods use different train/val/test splits and retraining protocols, producing widely varying absolute numbers for the *same* baseline (e.g., RoNIN-ResNet RoNIN-seen ATE appears as 3.99 m in CTIN's table but ~6 m in IMUNet/TinyIO splits). Treat percentage-improvement claims cautiously and prefer within-paper comparisons.
- **Preprint flags:** NeurIT (arXiv:2404.08939) and the original OxIOD (arXiv:1809.07491) remain non-peer-reviewed preprints; prefer the published OxIOD companion (arXiv:2001.04061 / IEEE-indexed L-IONet work) where a peer-reviewed citation is required.
- **The "CTIN reduces ATE ~46% / RTE ~32% over RoNIN" claim is contradicted by CTIN's own reported figures** (21.78% seen / 3.97% unseen average ATE over RoNIN ResNet). Do not propagate it.
- **CTIN is not a continuous-time model** despite informal secondary descriptions implying otherwise — verify your related-work text reflects this.
- **RoNIN author-order discrepancy** (Herath, Yan, Furukawa on IEEE vs. Yan, Herath, Furukawa on arXiv): cite the published IEEE order.
- One verified figure I could not pin to a single canonical number is **IMUNet's exact parameter count in millions**; it is firmly in the lightweight (<4.6 M) regime and matches RoNIN ResNet-18 accuracy, but confirm the precise figure from Table in arXiv:2208.00068 / the IEEE TIM version before quoting a specific number.
