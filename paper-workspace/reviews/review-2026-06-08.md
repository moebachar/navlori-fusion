## Verdict
Overall: **reject** (in current form; this is an early draft, not a submittable paper).

The paper proposes a single continuous-time set-transformer that fuses asynchronous WiFi and IMU as a time-stamped token set, with modality/instant dropout for missing-and-stale robustness, and reports a cross-session real-world result (10.89 m test MAE vs. 28.31 m for a WiFi baseline). The methodology and experiments sections are genuinely strong and well-argued, and the novelty framing in Related Work is unusually disciplined. But the paper has no abstract (literal `TODO`), no Introduction (only red author-note scaffolding), no Conclusion (literal `TODO`), and a figure flagged in its own caption as unreadable -- any one of these is a desk-reject trigger. The core science is promising; the artifact is not yet a paper.

## Findings

### Blockers
- [Content/Format] `\abstract{TODO}` -- the paper has no abstract. A reader (and the program chair's desk check) sees a placeholder. Blocker for submission.
- [Content/Format] Section 1 "Introduction" -- the body is two red `\textcolor{red}{...}` planning notes ("generalties, context, problem ...", "SOTA => organise your SOTA ..."). There is no Introduction. The paper jumps from title to Related Work. Blocker.
- [Content/Format] Section 6 "Conclusion" -- body is the literal word `TODO`. No conclusion, no future work. Blocker, and it means the intro-promise/conclusion-delivery check cannot even be run.
- [Format] Figure 1 caption -- ends with `\textcolor{red}{text is not visible: figure to be enhanced}`. The paper's one overview figure is, by the authors' own admission in the rendered caption, unreadable. A reviewer cannot evaluate the central architecture diagram. Blocker.
- [Format] Residual scaffolding visible in rendered output -- red `\textcolor{red}{...}` planning notes also sit at the head of Methodology (Section 3) and Experiments (Section 5). These render in the PDF. Any visible TODO/planning text is a submission blocker.

### Major
- [Content] "Methodology" / "Experiments" -- the text twice defers material to a "limitations discussion" (the Umeyama-aligned ATE that narrows the IMU gap; the rigid-body-aligned figure) that does not exist anywhere in the paper. As a reader I am promised the number that rescues the IMU leg's `+89.2%` deficit and never shown it. The single worst result in the paper is left hanging on a forward reference to absent text.
- [Content] No ablation supports the method's three headline mechanisms. The Related Work sells continuous-time Δt encoding, single-block set fusion, and token-level dropout as the contribution, but Experiments report only end-to-end MAE. There is no run with dropout off, no fixed-grid/resampling baseline, no staleness-vs-error curve. A reviewer will demand evidence that each claimed component earns its place; right now the "graceful degradation under stale sensors" claim (Related Work, Methodology) has zero supporting measurement in the paper.
- [Content] Comparative density is thin for a fusion paper. The only fusion-vs-external comparison is against `wlan_localization`, a WiFi-only fingerprinting baseline -- so "fusion beats WiFi" is partly tautological (it adds a modality the baseline lacks). No fusion baseline (e.g. an EKF or a late-concatenation deep model, both named in Related Work as the standard approaches) is run. The headline `-61.5%` is real but under-contextualized: it shows fusion beats WiFi-alone, not that this fusion design beats other fusion designs.
- [Content] Section 5.3 -- "both below the half-metre target" (Webots 0.395/0.375 m). The "half-metre target" is introduced as if established, but no target was set earlier in the paper (no Introduction exists to set it). As a reader I cannot tell where this success criterion comes from.

### Minor
- [Content] Section 5.1 / Table 4 (datasets) -- MSILN site1/B1 splits are "trajectories 94 / 34 / 5", yet the prose describes three day-separated sessions (train 24 Nov, val 25 Nov, test 5--6 Dec). A 5-trajectory test set carrying the entire headline claim is a small base; the paper does not acknowledge this. A reviewer will ask whether 10.89 m on 5 trajectories is a stable estimate.
- [Content] "MSILN site1/B1" is named as the headline dataset but never cited or described beyond its splits -- no source, no collection device, no reference. As a reader I cannot locate or trust this dataset; UJIIndoorLoc and RoNIN are cited, MSILN is not.
- [Format] Several uncited bib entries (52 of 76) -- harmless in the rendered PDF since apalike only prints cited works, but if any are meant to populate the still-unwritten Introduction, ensure they are actually cited; SCITEPRESS expects every listed reference to be cited.
- [Style] Section 5.1 -- "One named baseline per claim meets the venue's typical comparison density." This sentence argues with the reviewer inside the paper. It reads defensively and pre-empts an objection rather than answering it with evidence.
- [Format] Methodology cross-references use hard-coded section numbers ("deferred to Section 3.3", "Section 3.5", "Section 3.2") rather than `\ref`. If a section is added or reordered (e.g. the missing Introduction shifts numbering), every one of these silently breaks.

### Nits (batched)
- "generalties" (Section 1 red note) -- typo, but it is scaffolding that should not ship at all.
- Table 1 (`tab:arch`) caption "the conference 2-modality set-transformer" -- "conference" reads as internal shorthand (vs. a larger unpublished variant); a cold reader does not know what "the conference model" means.
- Equation 3 / WiFi-Net: `\mathbf{A} s` with `A` of shape `N_a x n_ap` implies `s` is the raw RSSI vector of length `n_ap`, but `n_ap` is never defined in the text (only `N_a`). Minor undefined symbol.
- Figure 1 is a `figure*` (full-width) placed in a `\onecolumn`... `\maketitle` context -- verify it floats correctly once the two-column body exists.
